Tricky day today. With Jonathan and Haroon both away, I'm the
only TPA developer on this side of the Atlantic so I have to
make sure I check newly raised tickets and new questions on
the Slack channel in the mornings. Today we had a couple of
newly raised tickets, which didn't take too long for me to
do enough thinking about, and a question which I spent rather
too long thinking about. Often users are confused (and often
developers are then confused) by bad error messages: not bad
in themselves, but if an error is caused by code silently
assuming some property of your cluster, then the error doesn't
mention that property, it just fails wherever that property
is first assumed in a way that actually causes a failure. The
lesson here is to be more willing to make assumptions explicit.
In my work on reconfiguration I'm trying to do that: a
transmogrifier in TPA is a multi-stage process, in which the
first stage is a check which can decide to abort the whole
operation, and the second actually makes the changes, so I'm
trying to make sure that anything that the second stage
assumes is actually checked for in the first stage.

I've had a couple of calls today, one with my manager, Martin,
and one with the whole team, or that part of it which isn't
on holiday. The team call can have a very different feeling
depending on who is here, who is running it, and what their
priorities are - it tends to expand to fill its hour, because
whoever is the most talkative person there can always talk
for as long as needed. Today we went over what we're currently
doing and who is taking care of what, which doesn't seem
like an hour's worth of call, and yet somehow we very nearly
got to the top of the hour. I didn't do a very good job of
getting back to concentrating on my work after it - I often
find this with afternoon calls.
